## 1. Executive Decision

WP-OP-01B approved 16 product archetypes. It did not create supplier-backed
products or catalog records. WP-OP-02 therefore treats every proposed name,
material, room, and Founder assessment as a production brief until documentary
or physical evidence confirms it.

The operating sequence is:

1. Stabilize Aurelia Modular Sofa and Calma Travertine Table against the full
   contract.
2. Remediate Nocturne Floor Lamp and Atelier Stone Vessel to reach four
   contract-complete products.
3. Produce the 16 approved WP-OP-01B products through three evidence-based
   batches.
4. Approve every product independently through Level 0, Level 1, Level 2, and
   Level 3.
5. Submit qualified Level 3 products separately for Level 4 and Founder review.

No proposed attribution, collection, batch membership, or Founder potential
constitutes exposure approval.

# B. Product Readiness and Production Matrix

## 4. Readiness Asset Contract

The existing readiness contract applies unchanged.

### 4.1 Identity and provenance

Every product requires:

- immutable `product_id`;
- approved EN and DE product names;
- reserved EN and DE slugs and PDP routes;
- canonical `VLS-{department}-{six digits}` SKU;
- supplier ID, supplier SKU, manufacturer, and source record;
- approved source batch, record owner, lifecycle state, and timestamps;
- explicit production classification, never demo or quarantine inheritance.

### 4.2 Commerce and fulfillment

Every product requires:

- positive gross and net prices;
- currency, tax class, and pricing approval;
- sellability status;
- availability source, stock policy, and verification timestamp;
- lead time, delivery class, returns class, and shipping constraints;
- dimensions, weight, units, assembly, care, safety, and compliance facts;
- active supplier, rollback target, and commerce reviewer.

Price and availability are mandatory for every product. A missing or
placeholder value blocks Level 1.

### 4.3 Content and media

Every product requires:

- factual EN and DE descriptions;
- EN and DE SEO titles and meta descriptions;
- at least five approved images:
  primary, alternate, detail, scale, and context;
- rights owner, scope, expiry, EN/DE alt text, crop profile, and quality
  approval for every image.

Installed, configurable, or materially variable products require additional
media where five images do not adequately disclose installation, options, or
variation.

### 4.4 Material authority

Every product requires:

- one canonical primary material and material family;
- all structurally, visibly, care-, safety-, or value-relevant secondary
  materials;
- finish and color;
- current supplier specification, bill of materials, certificate, or approved
  physical verification;
- `documented` or `verified` confidence before Level 3;
- approver and review date.

### 4.5 Discovery and governance

Every product requires:

- approved department, primary category, and product type;
- only evidence-supported secondary categories;
- at least one governed room or explicit `room_not_applicable`;
- relationship evidence, confidence, approver, and review date;
- resolved consultation mode and triggers;
- content, media, taxonomy, properties, commerce, supplier, and governance
  quality approvals;
- surface-specific exposure decisions and rollback targets.

## 5. Production Completeness Method

“Ready %” below is production-packet completeness, not readiness level.

Ten domains are weighted equally:

1. source identity;
2. commerce;
3. physical/compliance;
4. bilingual content;
5. media;
6. material evidence;
7. taxonomy;
8. room evidence;
9. consultation;
10. governance/exposure.

WP-OP-01B fully defines the intended taxonomy domain. All other domains remain
unverified or absent. Every product therefore begins at **10% production
completeness and pre-Level 0**, regardless of how strong its archetype is.

## 8. Material Acceptance Rules

The proposed material remains `unverified` until evidence is accepted.

- Product naming must change if evidence does not support the named material.
- `travertine`, `marble`, `wool`, `leather`, and oak claims require specific
  documentary or physical support.
- “Alabaster” must map to an approved canonical stone fact or be removed from
  the product name; imagery is insufficient.
- “Solid oak” must distinguish solid construction from veneer or oak-effect
  finish.
- Outdoor products require environmental suitability evidence in addition to
  composition.
- Upholstery records must capture fabric composition and materially significant
  fill or foam facts.
- Electrical BOMs do not replace material evidence, and material BOMs do not
  replace electrical compliance.

## 17. Production Workflow

For each product:

1. **Source acceptance:** validate supplier package and rights.
2. **Level 0 creation package:** assign internal ID, source, owner, batch, and
   lifecycle.
3. **Level 1 production:** produce identity, bilingual factual content,
   commerce, physical, compliance, fulfillment, care, and media records.
4. **Level 1 QA:** reject placeholders, contradictions, incomplete rights, and
   unsupported claims.
5. **Level 2 attribution:** map canonical taxonomy and materials; produce room
   evidence and bilingual SEO.
6. **Level 2 QA:** second-review relationships and controlled values.
7. **Level 3 preparation:** resolve consultation, publication, sellability,
   visibility, quality gates, and intended surfaces.
8. **Rendered validation:** check PDP and destination behavior in EN and DE.
9. **Exposure decision:** approve or reject every surface independently.
10. **Level 4 queue:** submit only qualified products for Founder review.

No partially complete batch is promoted as a unit. Products that pass may
advance while blocked products remain outside exposure.

- Consultation capacity must be confirmed before exposing products with
  `required` mode.

# 21. Risks and Controls

| Risk | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Working archetype is treated as a real product | Require supplier identity and source acceptance before Level 0 |
| Production percentage is mistaken for readiness | Display readiness level and completeness percentage separately |
| Attractive imagery outruns product facts | Source gate requires specifications, commerce facts, and rights |
| Named materials cannot be proven | Rename or reject the candidate; never infer from imagery |
| Proposed rooms become automatic tags | Require evidence and second approval per relationship |
| Batch deadlines weaken quality gates | Products advance independently; no batch waiver |
| New Arrivals is preassigned | Create membership only after Level 3 and governed availability date |
| Founder potential is treated as selection | Keep Future Candidate, Level 4 eligibility, and Founder decision separate |
| Optional consultation hides complexity | Re-evaluate mode from actual configuration, installation, and freight facts |
| Outdoor claims lack durability evidence | Block Level 2/3 until environmental suitability is documented |
| Supplier terminology changes taxonomy | Map to the approved product-type registry or reject |
| Translation introduces factual drift | Approve EN and DE against the same structured source facts |
