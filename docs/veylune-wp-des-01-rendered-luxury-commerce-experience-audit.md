# VEYLUNE STUDIO - WP-DES-01

## Rendered Luxury Commerce Experience Audit

**Audit date:** 7 June 2026  
**Scope:** Rendered storefront only  
**Viewports:** Desktop 1440 px; mobile 390 px  
**Method:** Direct visual review of the live local storefront, open-menu states, full-page captures, scrolled viewport captures, and representative destination pages.

This report does not score architecture, governance, code quality, or future intent. It scores only what a visitor can see and experience now.

## Executive Verdict

Veylune does **not** currently feel like 40% Westwing, 40% Apple, and 20% Veylune.

The present rendered ratio is approximately:

| Influence | Current Share |
| --- | ---: |
| Westwing | 10% |
| Apple | 15% |
| Veylune | 75% |

The high Veylune share is not yet a success. It reflects a strong but overextended house style dominating screens that lack the commercial completeness of Westwing and the precision, restraint, and functional confidence of Apple.

The storefront has several credible luxury ingredients: restrained color, large editorial typography, atmospheric imagery, material-focused language, and a distinctive gallery tone. Those ingredients are repeatedly undermined by visible error content, empty catalog destinations, internal governance language, excessive blank space, weak utility hierarchy, repetitive product exposure, and non-functional commerce cues.

The most damaging visible fact is that every audited route displays a large **"Page not found"** message in the header region. A visitor encounters failure before proposition, product, or trust.

The experience currently resembles an unfinished luxury editorial prototype with commerce controls attached. It does not resemble a mature luxury commerce platform.

# PART II - Header Audit

**Header score: 18/100**

## Findings

- A large "Page not found" block occupies the right side of the desktop header on every audited page.
- The error block is visually stronger than the logo, search, account, and cart hierarchy.
- The logo is small, pale, and visually recessive.
- The brand is expressed three times in the same region: navigation logo, "VEYLUNE STUDIO," and error copy.
- Primary navigation is pushed to the far left while utilities and failure content overload the center-right.
- Search is present but visually slight and easy to miss.
- No cart control is visible in the audited desktop header despite product cards presenting add-to-cart actions.
- Account appears as an unlabeled icon.
- Language and currency controls float above the main utility row and create a fragmented two-level header.
- The utility controls use boxed borders that feel more administrative than luxurious.
- The header is unusually tall because failure content has expanded it.
- The desktop spacing produces three disconnected zones rather than one balanced navigation system.
- On mobile, the logo is compressed into a very small horizontal mark.
- On mobile, "VEYLUNE STUDIO" and the 404 panel consume the top-right column, making the header feel malformed.
- Mobile search is reduced to a tiny icon without supporting context.
- Mobile utility actions are incomplete or absent.
- The hamburger, logo, and search icon do not form a convincing aligned control row.
- Sticky behavior does not create a consistently useful commerce utility layer during the audited scroll states.

# PART III - Mega Menu Audit

**Mega menu score: 24/100**

## Findings

- The menu can occupy a broad central panel, but the persistent 404 block remains layered above it.
- The error panel overlaps the menu's upper-right reading area.
- Background content remains visibly present and visually noisy behind the open menu.
- The menu has weak separation from the header and page beneath it.
- Column headings and links lack a decisive hierarchy.
- Link rows are spacious but visually generic.
- The panel feels like a taxonomy table rather than a luxury discovery surface.
- There is no visible imagery, featured edit, product highlight, or editorial cue.
- There is no visible active-path clarity beyond a small underline.
- The three columns are mechanically balanced but not compositionally balanced.
- The panel's pale background merges with the page background.
- Layering makes the menu look partially trapped beneath another interface.
- The menu exposes breadth but not desirability.
- Small uppercase navigation labels and large serif menu links feel disconnected.
- The overall state reads as structurally compromised rather than intentionally immersive.

# PART VIII - Typography Audit

**Typography score: 46/100**

## Strengths

- The serif display face is distinctive and suitable for an editorial interiors identity.
- Small uppercase labels create a consistent archival tone.
- Dark charcoal on warm white is generally comfortable.
- Product names are legible and visually coherent with the brand.

## Problems

- Display headings are repeatedly oversized beyond their information value.
- Many headings break into awkward line lengths and dominate entire viewports.
- The same large-serif device is used so often that hierarchy flattens.
- Very small uppercase labels have low practical readability.
- Letter spacing on utility and microcopy is excessive at small sizes.
- Body copy becomes faint against the warm background.
- The sans-serif error panel is visually louder than the intended brand typography.
- Product microcopy is crowded and inconsistent in emphasis.
- Mobile type scaling is uneven: headings remain dominant while controls and metadata become tiny.
- Typography often communicates editorial posture where commerce clarity is needed.

## Westwing Comparison

Westwing's current public experience visibly presents broad category access, active product merchandising, explicit prices, campaign content, design service, payment confidence, delivery confidence, and service links. Veylune presents the labels for a commerce system but little of the rendered proof that the system is populated and dependable.

The Westwing gap is largest in:

- Assortment density
- Category credibility
- Product variety
- Campaign merchandising
- Service and trust visibility
- Conversion continuity
- Commercial freshness

## Apple Comparison

Apple's current public experience presents short propositions, focused images, direct actions, consistent modules, and complete destinations. Veylune shares Apple's preference for visual restraint and large imagery, but not its control of density, action hierarchy, functional completeness, or error-free polish.

The Apple gap is largest in:

- First-screen clarity
- Functional confidence
- Concision
- Consistent hierarchy
- Purposeful white space
- Action prominence
- Mobile control

## Veylune Identity Assessment

The visible identity is distinctive but over-applied. Material restraint, gallery language, warm neutrals, and quiet editorial typography are credible. The problem is not absence of identity. The problem is that identity is carrying screens that lack sufficient product, service, and commerce evidence.

# PART XIV - Final Verdict

## Would the experience feel like 40% Westwing, 40% Apple, and 20% Veylune?

No.

It contains approximately 10% Westwing in department breadth and basic product-card commerce; 15% Apple in restraint, large imagery, and minimal visual vocabulary; and 75% Veylune in tone, typography, spacing, and editorial posture.

The intended ratio is not achieved because the Westwing component lacks assortment and merchandising, while the Apple component lacks precision and functional completeness. Veylune identity therefore operates without the two stabilizing influences that were meant to make it commercially convincing.

## What Westwing Executives Would Criticize

- The visible lack of assortment.
- Empty category, room, and collection destinations.
- Reuse of two products across most commercial surfaces.
- No convincing category merchandising.
- No visible filters, sorting, product count, or assortment depth.
- Weak product-card conversion hierarchy.
- Missing trust, delivery, payment, and service confidence.
- Internal governance language displayed to customers.
- A homepage that spends more space explaining its posture than presenting purchasable choice.
- The persistent 404 content as a severe commercial credibility failure.

## What Apple Retail Executives Would Criticize

- A visible error state occupying the global header.
- Lack of first-screen clarity.
- White space without controlled purpose.
- Oversized typography used without disciplined hierarchy.
- Inconsistent product imagery.
- Ambiguous actions and contradictory commerce states.
- A fragmented header with weak utility balance.
- Destinations that are visibly incomplete.
- Mobile compression of already-compromised header content.
- Too much explanatory language and too little decisive action.

## What They Would Praise

- A recognizable point of view.
- A restrained and consistent color world.
- Several atmospheric image choices.
- A typography system with genuine character.
- A clear interest in material, proportion, and spatial context.
- The absence of noisy marketplace styling.
- The potential for a differentiated editorial-commerce identity.

## Final Answer

A visitor landing on Veylune today would not perceive a mature luxury commerce platform. They would perceive a visually intentional but unfinished gallery storefront whose visible failures and empty destinations outweigh its strongest aesthetic moments.

The experience is not currently Westwing-grade in commerce, Apple-grade in precision, or fully Veylune-grade in authority. It demonstrates identity potential, but visible reality communicates incompleteness.
