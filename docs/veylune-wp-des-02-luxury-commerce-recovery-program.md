# VEYLUNE STUDIO - WP-DES-02

## Luxury Commerce Recovery Program

**Program date:** 7 June 2026  
**Source:** WP-DES-01 Rendered Luxury Commerce Experience Audit  
**Scope:** Prioritization only  
**Baseline:** 28/100 rendered-experience score

This program uses only the 50 documented WP-DES-01 problems. It does not add new findings, prescribe implementation, or redesign the storefront.

## Executive Decision

Ten issues account for most of the perceived-quality loss. They fall into four recovery obligations:

1. Remove visible evidence of failure.
2. Stop presenting visibly empty destinations as active commerce.
3. Establish enough product and journey proof to make the storefront commercially credible.
4. Restore hierarchy and density so the existing identity reads as controlled luxury.

The first recovery wave is not a visual-polish wave. It is a credibility wave.

The realistic progression is:

| State | Experience Score |
| --- | ---: |
| Current | 28/100 |
| After Wave 1 | 42/100 |
| After Wave 2 | 54/100 |
| After Wave 3 | 62/100 |

Reaching 60+ does not require resolving all 50 findings. It requires eliminating the most visible signs of failure and incompleteness, then making the existing commerce surfaces coherent.

| 9 | 9 | Homepage/spacing | Extreme blank vertical regions look like missing or failed content. |

## Why These Ten

These issues have one or more of the following characteristics:

- They appear globally or across several destinations.
- They communicate failure rather than merely weak taste.
- They prevent visitors from trusting product availability or purchase intent.
- They make suppliers question whether the platform is operational.
- They undermine several audit scores at once.
- Their removal improves the perceived quality of existing content without requiring a new visual identity.

## Issues Not in the Critical Ten

The other 40 findings remain valid. They are deferred because they are:

- Consequences of the critical ten.
- Localized to one component.
- Primarily polish issues.
- Lower-impact while visible failure and emptiness remain.
- Unable to materially improve supplier confidence on their own.

Examples that can wait include the cookie banner's visual weight, back-to-top styling, footer context, small uppercase readability, account labeling, material-chip prominence, and bordered shortcut styling.

The first five issues damage basic confidence: whether the site works, whether products exist, whether product details can be reached, and whether an item is actually purchasable.

Suppliers are likely to interpret visible errors, empty departments, repeated products, and ambiguous commerce controls as evidence that the platform is not ready to represent their products professionally.

- An unreliable product journey.
- Unclear purchase versus inquiry status.
- Empty commercial destinations.
- Insufficient product breadth.
- Weak product-card action hierarchy.

# PART III - Root Cause Analysis

## Root-Cause Distribution

| Root Cause | Critical Issues Explained | Weight |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Empty states | 1, 3, 7, 8 | 25% |
| Product visibility and merchandising | 3, 4, 7, 8 | 25% |
| Commerce hierarchy and journey clarity | 2, 5, 10 | 20% |
| Placeholder/internal content | 6 | 10% |
| Spacing and visual density | 9 | 10% |
| Header hierarchy | 1 | 5% |
| Navigation | Secondary consequence of 1 and 2 | 3% |
| Typography | Amplifies 6 and 9 but is not the primary cause | 2% |

## True Root Causes

### 1. Public Surfaces Expose Non-Public States

WP-DES-01 issues 1, 6, 7, and 8 share the same perceptual cause: visitors see system failure, governance status, or future population states that do not belong in a finished luxury-commerce experience.

This is the largest source of avoidable quality loss.

### 2. The Storefront Does Not Show Enough Commercial Proof

Issues 3, 4, 7, and 8 are not primarily typography or layout problems. They are merchandising and product-visibility problems. Empty or repeated product streams cannot become commercially credible through polish alone.

### 3. Purchase Intent Is Not Coherent

Issues 2, 5, and 10 make it difficult to understand the path from product discovery to product evaluation and purchase. The storefront presents commerce controls without one clear, dependable commerce state.

### 4. Editorial Restraint Has Become Visual Absence

Issue 9 is the main spacing root cause. The audit did not reject white space as a principle. It found that its current scale resembles missing content and breaks visual continuity.

### 5. Identity Is Carrying Too Much Operational Weakness

Oversized typography, quiet colors, and gallery language are not the primary problem. They amplify empty states and weak merchandising because those elements occupy space that would otherwise contain product proof.

## What Is Not a Primary Root Cause

| Area | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Layout | Important amplifier, not the dominant cause |
| Hierarchy | Critical within header and product cards, but not the source of empty destinations |
| Navigation | Structurally present; damaged mainly by the global error state and weak destination outcomes |
| Typography | Overused, but not responsible for the platform appearing non-operational |
| Spacing | Major homepage problem, but secondary to visible errors and empty commerce |
| Visual density | Too low in key areas; caused largely by missing product and content proof |

# PART IV - Recovery Sequence

## Wave 1 - Credibility Recovery

**Objective:** Stop the storefront from looking broken or internally unfinished.

| Order | Critical Issue | Recovery Condition |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Global "Page not found" state | No normal audited storefront screen visibly communicates route failure. |
| 2 | Internal governance language | Customer-facing pages no longer describe governance, registries, approval shells, or future population. |
| 3 | Empty primary departments | Active department destinations no longer present visible empty-commerce states. |
| 4 | Empty room destinations | Active room destinations no longer present visible empty shells. |
| 5 | Empty permanent/editorial collections | Active collection destinations no longer advertise absence. |

### Expected Perceptual Effect

- The storefront stops declaring its own incompleteness.
- Header, menu, hero, category, room, and collection screens all benefit.
- Supplier visitors encounter a controlled public surface rather than operational scaffolding.
- Existing brand assets become more credible without changing the identity system.

## Wave 2 - Commerce Proof Recovery

**Objective:** Make the visible store behave like a coherent product business.

| Order | Critical Issue | Recovery Condition |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 6 | No dependable product-detail journey | Every exposed product has a visibly dependable evaluation destination. |
| 7 | Two products repeated across surfaces | Discovery surfaces demonstrate enough distinct product breadth to support their labels. |
| 8 | Inquiry and cart contradiction | Every product presents one understandable commerce state. |
| 9 | Confused product-card hierarchy | Product identity, price, availability, shipping information, and primary action read in a clear order. |

### Expected Perceptual Effect

- Product discovery begins to lead somewhere credible.
- New Arrivals, Founder Selection, categories, and rooms stop feeling like duplicated labels.
- Revenue intent becomes understandable.
- Suppliers can see how their products would be represented and evaluated.

## Wave 3 - Luxury Control Recovery

**Objective:** Convert the existing editorial identity from unfinished spaciousness into controlled restraint.

| Order | Critical Issue | Recovery Condition |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 10 | Extreme blank vertical regions | Homepage spacing no longer resembles missing content or failed rendering. |

Wave 3 also absorbs the direct consequences of this issue already identified in WP-DES-01:

- Excessive homepage length.
- Broken visual rhythm.
- Editorial statements delaying product discovery.
- Oversized hierarchy dominating weak content.

These remain one recovery priority because they originate from the same density and pacing problem.

### Expected Perceptual Effect

- Strong image sections connect into a continuous experience.
- White space reads as intentional.
- Product and discovery content appears earlier and with greater momentum.
- The Apple influence becomes more credible through control rather than imitation.

## Why Wave 1 Increases Apple Influence

Apple influence in WP-DES-01 was defined by functional confidence, concise propositions, complete destinations, and controlled hierarchy. Removing global error content and internal-state messaging improves those qualities before any aesthetic polish occurs.

## Why Wave 2 Increases Westwing Influence

Westwing influence was limited mainly by insufficient assortment, weak product discovery, empty commercial destinations, and unclear conversion continuity. Product breadth and a dependable journey directly recover those qualities.

## Why Wave 3 Reduces the Excess Veylune Share

The current 75% Veylune share reflects an identity system overexposed against too little commerce content. Better pacing and density allow the Veylune layer to remain distinctive without dominating the entire experience.

The intended 40/40/20 ratio is not projected after these waves because category merchandising, service trust, menu depth, mobile refinement, and broader commerce maturity remain incomplete.

# PART X - Final Recommendation

## The Ten Issues to Resolve Before Supplier Outreach

The ten issues should be addressed in this order:

1. Global "Page not found" state.
2. Internal governance language on customer-facing pages.
3. Empty primary department destinations.
4. Empty room destinations.
5. Empty Permanent and Editorial Collection destinations.
6. Undependable product-detail journey.
7. Two-product repetition across commercial surfaces.
8. Inquiry and cart contradiction.
9. Confused product-card information and action hierarchy.
10. Extreme homepage blank regions and broken pacing.

## Why This Order

### First: Remove Failure

Issues 1 and 2 make the platform visibly unfit for supplier review regardless of its other strengths. They are also the highest-return recovery actions.

### Second: Remove Public Emptiness

Issues 3 through 5 determine whether discovery destinations look active or theoretical. A supplier must not encounter category, room, or collection pages that advertise missing population.

### Third: Prove Commerce

Issues 6 through 9 determine whether products can be discovered, evaluated, and acted upon coherently. These are necessary for commercial credibility and revenue confidence.

### Fourth: Recover Luxury Rhythm

Issue 10 should follow credibility recovery. Better spacing cannot compensate for visible failure or insufficient merchandise, but once those problems are controlled, pacing produces a substantial gain in perceived finish.

## What Can Be Ignored for Now

The following WP-DES-01 findings should not consume recovery capacity before the critical ten:

- Cookie-banner visual competition.
- Back-to-top styling.
- Footer context.
- Language and currency styling.
- Account icon labeling.
- Small uppercase readability.
- Body-copy contrast refinements.
- Material-chip prominence.
- Bordered shortcut styling.
- "View record" terminology.
- Truncated card descriptions.
- Secondary mega-menu hierarchy.

These remain legitimate issues, but none can independently move Veylune from 28/100 toward 60/100.

## Final Decision

Veylune does not need a complete redesign to cross 60/100. It needs disciplined removal of visible failure and emptiness, followed by credible product proof and controlled visual density.

The recovery target is:

**28/100 -> 42/100 -> 54/100 -> 62/100**

Supplier outreach should evaluate the rendered result after Wave 2. At that point, the projected Supplier Impression score reaches approximately 64/100. Wave 3 raises polish and perceived luxury, but Waves 1 and 2 establish the minimum commercial credibility.

No lower-priority styling issue should displace the ten priorities in this program.
