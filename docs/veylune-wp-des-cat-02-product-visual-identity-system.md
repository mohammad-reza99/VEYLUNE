# VEYLUNE STUDIO - WP-DES-CAT-02

## Product Visual Identity System

**Status:** Governed visual direction  
**Scope:** 50 draft catalog products  
**Source of truth:** WP-CAT-01, WP-CAT-02, WP-CAT-03, WP-CAT-04, and the WP-IMP-CAT-01 draft catalog manifest  
**Implementation status:** Documentation only  
**Storefront, Shopware entities, and database:** Unchanged

---

## Executive Decision

Veylune should not solve the current placeholder effect by producing fifty unrelated lifestyle images. It should establish one recognizable visual world with controlled variation by product family.

The system is:

1. **One gallery:** every image belongs to the same warm, architectural Veylune environment.
2. **Distinct family grammars:** furniture is grounded, lighting is luminous, objects are sculptural, textiles are tactile, dining is planar, and outdoor is environmental.
3. **Immediate object recognition:** silhouette must be readable before styling, copy, or context.
4. **Material before decoration:** light, angle, and crop must explain the material construction.
5. **Controlled hierarchy:** hero products receive spatial context and stronger scale; supporting products receive quieter, repeatable studio compositions.

The intended result is not a uniform catalog. It is a coherent collection in which each family has a different visual behavior.

---

## Governing Principles

### Apple-Inspired Product Presentation

The following principles are translated for Veylune rather than copied:

- **Product first:** the object occupies the highest-contrast and most legible area of the frame.
- **Image dominance:** the primary image communicates product type, silhouette, and proportion without relying on text.
- **Minimal distractions:** props exist only to establish scale, use, or material relationship.
- **Strong recognition:** no primary image hides defining arms, bases, openings, edges, or profiles.
- **Consistent alignment:** comparable products share horizon, visual center, and crop logic.
- **Purposeful context:** room scenes clarify use; they do not become interior editorials.
- **One message per image:** silhouette, material, scale, or use is assigned deliberately to each asset.

### Veylune-Specific Principles

- **Quiet Architectural Luxury:** calm geometry, restrained rooms, and deliberate negative space.
- **Material-first presentation:** grain, veining, weave, patina, translucency, and joinery are visible.
- **Warm ivory palette:** backgrounds remain warm and mineral, never clinical white.
- **Soft shadow system:** shadows provide weight and shape without hard commercial cutout edges.
- **Gallery-quality composition:** each frame is balanced enough to stand alone while remaining useful for commerce.
- **Honest draft status:** imagery must not imply an approved supplier, exact finish, production-ready construction, or deliverable stock.
- **No decorative camouflage:** styling must never compensate for an unresolved product silhouette.

# 1. Product Family Visual System

## 1.1 Shared Visual World

All product families operate inside one controlled visual environment:

| Component | Governing rule |
|---|---|
| Palette | Warm ivory, limestone, oat, clay, muted oak, aged metal, smoke glass, charcoal accents |
| Architecture | Plaster planes, stone floors, oak panels, restrained reveals, broad openings |
| Light | Large directional source, warm-neutral white balance, soft falloff, material-readable highlights |
| Styling | Maximum three supporting objects; none may compete with the product |
| Human presence | Normally absent; permitted only as a cropped scale cue for selected seating, lighting, and outdoor assets |
| Color behavior | Low saturation with enough separation to preserve silhouette |
| Retouching | Natural texture retained; no excessive smoothing, bloom, sharpening, or artificial gloss |
| Draft integrity | Images communicate direction, not supplier-confirmed construction claims |

## 1.2 Family Grammars

| Family | Visual character | Primary setting | Camera behavior | Material behavior | Differentiation objective |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Furniture | Grounded, architectural, inhabitable | Studio-room threshold | Low to eye-level three-quarter views | Grain, upholstery depth, edge profile, base weight | Communicate proportion and physical authority |
| Lighting | Luminous, graphic, atmospheric | Controlled studio or dusk interior | Straight or slight low angle | Glow, diffusion, metal finish, stone or ceramic body | Make light emission and fixture silhouette equally legible |
| Decor & Objects | Sculptural, collectible, precise | Mineral plinth or restrained shelf | Slightly elevated three-quarter view | Surface variation, carving, casting, rim, and mass | Give each object a distinct profile and collectible presence |
| Textiles & Rugs | Tactile, soft, layered | Floor plane or quiet textile studio | Top-down, grazing, or low oblique view | Weave, pile, edge, drape, compression | Replace silhouette similarity with texture and movement |
| Dining & Kitchen | Planar, functional, composed | Dining architecture or clean tabletop | Elevated three-quarter and near top-down | Top surface, rim, joinery, ceramic glaze, wood grain | Explain use without becoming a styled food campaign |
| Outdoor | Environmental, durable, monolithic | Courtyard, terrace, or garden wall | Eye-level or low architectural view | Weatherable texture, teak grain, stone mass, woven exterior surface | Establish exterior scale and environmental credibility |

## 1.3 Required Scene Mix

No product relies on a single image mode. The primary mode is selected by product type.

| Mode | Definition | Required use |
|---|---|---|
| **IS - Isolated studio** | Product on a warm mineral background with controlled contact shadow | Primary for decor, small lighting, dining accessories, and supporting furniture |
| **FR - Full-room scene** | Product shown within a complete but restrained room | Primary for sofas, beds, large rugs, and selected dining furniture |
| **AC - Architectural context** | Product placed against meaningful architecture without a fully styled room | Primary for mirrors, consoles, wall lights, outdoor, and selected storage |
| **MD - Material detail** | Close crop explaining texture, edge, joinery, glow, weave, or finish | Required secondary for every product |
| **SC - Scale/context** | Image with architecture or a restrained familiar object to establish dimensions | Required for products whose size is ambiguous in isolation |

# 3. Product Hierarchy Framework

## 3.1 Hierarchy Levels

### H1 - Hero Products

Hero products establish the visual authority of a family or collection.

**Image allocation:** 6-8 governed images  
**Primary image:** FR or AC where the product benefits from scale; exceptional IS for sculptural products  
**Frame occupancy:** 62-78% of the usable frame  
**Context:** purpose-built, family-defining environment  
**Motion/video readiness:** required framing allowance for future slow orbit or material film  
**Homepage role:** eligible for first rail position, large feature tile, or collection lead

### H2 - Anchor Products

Anchor products provide comparison depth and reinforce the family language.

**Image allocation:** 4-6 governed images  
**Primary image:** IS or AC  
**Frame occupancy:** 58-72%  
**Context:** one controlled scale image  
**Homepage role:** eligible for standard rail positions and destination headers

### H3 - Supporting Products

Supporting products extend assortment without competing with category anchors.

**Image allocation:** 3-4 governed images  
**Primary image:** repeatable IS system  
**Frame occupancy:** 54-68%  
**Context:** optional unless scale is ambiguous  
**Homepage role:** rail depth, category discovery, and collection completion

## 3.2 Hero Selection Rules

A product qualifies as H1 only if it meets at least four of these conditions:

- It has an immediately recognizable silhouette.
- It represents a strategically important product family.
- It carries material authority at thumbnail size.
- It can establish scale through architecture.
- It creates strong contrast with neighboring rail products.
- It has Founder Selection potential.
- It can lead a room or collection without relying on accessory styling.

Hero status is visual, not commercial. It does not imply supplier approval or activation.

## 3.4 Rail Image Rhythm

A rail should not contain six images with the same crop, angle, height, and background value. Governed rail sequencing uses:

1. One H1 contextual image.
2. One isolated three-quarter object.
3. One lighter or more open silhouette.
4. One vertical product.
5. One material-rich close form.
6. One supporting product with restrained composition.

Products remain aligned by card anatomy, but visual rhythm changes through silhouette and image composition.

# 5. Image Composition Standards

## 5.1 Asset Set Per Product

Every governed product should ultimately receive:

1. **Primary recognition image:** strongest full silhouette and family-specific composition.
2. **Material detail image:** texture, finish, joinery, edge, weave, glow, or construction focus.
3. **Scale/context image:** architecture or restrained familiar relationship.
4. **Alternate angle:** required for H1 and H2 products; optional for H3.
5. **State image:** open/closed, illuminated/unlit, arranged/unarranged, or draped/flat when relevant.

No asset should be produced merely to reach an image count.

## 5.2 Frame and Crop Standards

| Use | Master ratio | Product occupancy | Safe area |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rail/card primary | 4:5 master with center-safe 1:1 crop | 58-76% | Keep defining silhouette inside central 82% |
| Category grid | 4:5 | 56-74% | Maintain common baseline by product type |
| Wide feature | 16:9 | 45-68% | Reserve 20-30% quiet space for layout flexibility |
| Full-room hero | 3:2 or 16:9 | Product controls 35-60% of visual attention | Product must remain recognizable at card crop |
| Detail image | 1:1 or 4:5 | 70-92% | Texture remains legible without losing material context |

Master images must be captured with enough margin to support 4:5, 1:1, and 16:9 derivatives without generative extension.

## 5.3 Alignment Standards

- Seating feet or base align to a consistent lower image band.
- Pendant canopies and suspension are never cropped from the master.
- Tall storage, mirrors, floor lamps, and planters retain visible top and base.
- Table tops use a repeatable elevated viewpoint within each table type.
- Vessels show their openings; trays show their interior planes.
- Rugs show at least three edges in context and all four edges in the isolated asset.
- Transparent glass is separated from the background by controlled edge contrast, not fake outlines.

## 5.4 Lens and Perspective Direction

- Use normal-to-short-telephoto perspective for isolated products.
- Avoid wide-angle distortion in furniture and room scenes.
- Keep vertical architecture corrected.
- Camera height follows the product's functional center, not a generic standing eye level.
- Full frontal views are reserved for mirrors, storage elevations, wall lights, and geometry that benefits from precision.
- Three-quarter angles must reveal construction, not simply add visual drama.

## 5.5 Material Lighting Direction

| Material | Lighting requirement | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Travertine and stone | Broad raking light that reveals pores and vein direction | Flat frontal light or excessive contrast |
| Oak and teak | Directional light along grain with controlled warm balance | Orange color cast or over-sharpened grain |
| Leather | Large source showing natural creases and edge construction | Plastic highlights or perfectly smooth surfaces |
| Upholstery and boucle | Side-biased soft light revealing depth and weave | Front-flat light that erases texture |
| Ceramic | Gradient reflection defining volume and glaze variation | Hard specular spots or artificial uniformity |
| Glass | Dark/light cards or architectural planes creating edge definition | Busy reflected rooms and clipped highlights |
| Metal | Long controlled reflection line showing contour and finish | Uncontrolled room reflections or mirror-like chaos |
| Textiles and rugs | Low grazing component plus soft fill | Deep crushed shadows that hide weave |

## 5.6 Shadow System

| Code | Shadow type | Application |
|---|---|---|
| S1 Gallery Contact | Compact, soft, directionally clear | Decor, table lamps, dining accessories |
| S2 Grounded Architectural | Broad contact with dense core and soft perimeter | Sofas, chairs, tables, storage, beds |
| S3 Luminous Cast | Physical contact plus controlled emitted pool or wall wash | All lighting |
| S4 Textile Grazing | Fine texture shadows with low-contrast grounding | Rugs, runners, throws, cushions |
| S5 Exterior Daylight | Directional natural shadow softened during grading | Outdoor products |
| S6 Transparent Layer | Subtle layered shadow or caustic preserving material honesty | Glass furniture and glass lighting |

Shadows may not be copied as identical generic overlays across products. Direction, density, and footprint must agree with the object and scene.

## 5.7 Background Palette

| Background | Reference value | Primary use |
|---|---|---|
| Gallery Ivory | `#F3EFE6` | General isolated products |
| Oat Plaster | `#E7DED0` | Pale stone, glass, lighting, and room architecture |
| Limestone | `#D4C8B7` | Dark furniture, metal, and leather |
| Warm Mineral Grey | `#BDB6AB` | Ivory ceramics, glass, and light upholstery |
| Evening Taupe | `#4B453E` | Illuminated lighting assets only |
| Courtyard Sand | `#CDBB9F` | Outdoor architecture |

These are directional production references, not mandatory digital backdrop fills. Final values must adapt to the product while retaining a warm-neutral family relationship.

## 5.8 Styling Limits

- Primary isolated images: zero props.
- Primary contextual images: maximum three supporting elements.
- Scale images: one clear scale cue is preferred over multiple decorative cues.
- No books with visible brands, staged food, flowers as focal points, fashion accessories, candles, or art that competes with the product.
- Plants are restricted to outdoor context and occasional architectural scale, using quiet forms and muted green.
- Neighboring Veylune products may appear in context only when the featured product remains unmistakably dominant.

## 5.9 Draft and AI Visualization Governance

Before supplier onboarding, visualizations must remain directional:

- Do not imply exact material sourcing, engineering, finish tolerance, or production construction.
- Do not add supplier marks or invented provenance.
- Do not depict mechanisms that are absent from the governed record.
- Preserve each record's status as **Coming Soon** or **Supplier Selection**.
- Maintain a product-to-image register recording prompt/source, revision, approval state, intended use, and supersession.
- Replace directional renders with supplier-approved imagery through a controlled asset review, not silent substitution.

# 6. Draft Catalog Upgrade Roadmap

## Phase 0 - Identity Lock

**Purpose:** prevent another round of visually similar placeholders.

- Approve the six family grammars.
- Approve the background and shadow systems.
- Approve H1, H2, and H3 hierarchy assignments.
- Confirm the defining silhouette and material claim for every product.
- Create a visual reference board by product type, not only by brand mood.

**Exit condition:** every product has an approved one-line visual brief and assigned image mode.

## Phase 1 - First 20 Identity Set

**Purpose:** make the preview immediately read as a broad luxury ecosystem.

- Produce the ranked first 20 products in Section 7.
- Cover all six departments.
- Produce all H1 primary assets included in the first 20.
- Test rail rhythm using real differences in silhouette, height, material, and context.
- Review at card size before approving high-resolution details.

**Exit condition:** homepage and primary destinations no longer depend on repeated generic studies.

## Phase 2 - Family Completion

**Purpose:** establish comparison depth within product families.

- Complete remaining H2 anchors.
- Produce matched-angle comparisons for chairs, coffee tables, floor lamps, table lamps, vessels, and cushions.
- Ensure every product family has at least one contextual lead and three isolated comparison assets.
- Correct any family whose backgrounds or crops have drifted.

**Exit condition:** users can distinguish products inside a family without reading names.

## Phase 3 - Supporting Assortment

**Purpose:** complete the 50-product system efficiently.

- Produce H3 products through repeatable isolated studio setups.
- Add scale images only where dimensions remain ambiguous.
- Complete material details for all records.
- Validate color, shadow, and baseline consistency across category grids.

**Exit condition:** all 50 records have a complete minimum asset set.

## Phase 4 - Supplier Transition

**Purpose:** replace directional visualization with approved product truth.

- Reconcile silhouettes and dimensions with supplier samples.
- Validate materials, color, construction, and finish.
- Reshoot or rerender products whose governed visual identity remains valid but geometry changes.
- Archive superseded draft assets.
- Preserve the family grammar while increasing factual accuracy.

**Exit condition:** no public-facing asset makes an unverified product claim.

## Phase 5 - Production Quality Control

Run three review passes:

1. **Recognition review:** can the product type and silhouette be identified at thumbnail size?
2. **Family review:** does the asset belong clearly to its family while remaining Veylune?
3. **Commerce review:** can users compare shape, material, and scale without decorative interference?

Reject an image if it is attractive but fails any of these tests.

## Acceptance Criteria

The Product Visual Identity System is successful when:

- Furniture, lighting, decor, textiles, dining, and outdoor are distinguishable without headings.
- Sofas, tables, mirrors, vessels, and storage do not share interchangeable placeholder compositions.
- Every product has a readable defining silhouette at card size.
- Materials remain visible and credible without false supplier claims.
- Hero products carry greater spatial and visual authority than supporting products.
- Rails feel varied but not inconsistent.
- The warm ivory, architectural Veylune world remains recognizable across all image modes.
- Product images function as commerce assets first and editorial images second.
